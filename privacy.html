Qnote privacy policy

Effective 10 November 2026

This policy covers the Qnote note-taking app for Windows and Android. The short version: Qnote has no server, and its developer never receives your notes or your Google data. They stay on your devices and in your own Google Drive.

Google data Qnote accesses

If you choose to connect Google Drive, Qnote asks for one permission, drive.file. It lets Qnote see, create, change and delete only the files Qnote itself created in your Drive. Qnote can't see any other file in your Drive, and it doesn't ask for your name, email address, contacts or anything else from your Google account.

How Qnote uses that data

Qnote uses this access only to sync your notebooks between your own devices. It doesn't use your data for advertising, doesn't sell it, and has no analytics or tracking of any kind.

On Windows, spelling is checked by Windows' own spell checker on your computer. Your text isn't sent anywhere for it.

Qnote's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Who it is shared with

No one. Your data goes directly between your devices and Google's servers. The developer has no access to it, and nothing is transferred to any other party.

What is stored, and where

On your device:

  • Your notebooks, pages and pictures, in Qnote's folder.
  • The words you add to Qnote's spelling dictionary, in Qnote's folder.
  • Your Google sign-in and any GitHub token you add, in the system's secure keychain (Windows Credential Manager or the Android Keystore).

In your Google Drive, if you connect it, in a folder named Qnote:

  • Your notebooks, pages and pictures.
  • The words you add to Qnote's spelling dictionary, so every device knows them. This list is not encrypted, even for words added while writing in a private notebook.
  • A name and a random identifier for each of your devices. The name is shown when two devices change the same text, to say which version came from where.

How your data is protected

  • Everything travels to and from Google over an encrypted connection.
  • Private notebooks are encrypted on your device before they are saved or synced. The password never leaves your device, so anyone with access to your Drive gets only encrypted files. They can still see that a private notebook exists, the label you gave it, and how many files it has and roughly how big they are.
  • Ordinary notebooks are not encrypted by Qnote. In your Drive they have the protection of your Google account.
  • Location and other hidden details are removed from pictures when you add them.

Checking for updates

Each time it starts, Qnote asks GitHub whether a newer version has been released, using a read-only token that you provide. If there is one and you choose to install it, Qnote downloads it from GitHub. None of your notes or Google data are sent, though GitHub, like any website, can see the request and its network address. GitHub's own privacy statement applies to that request. Without a token, Qnote doesn't make this check.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays until you delete it.

When you delete a page or notebook in Qnote it disappears from the app, but its contents are still kept on your device and in your Google Drive. Qnote doesn't currently remove them. To remove them for good, follow the steps below.

Pictures no page has used for 30 days are removed automatically. Files Qnote removes from Drive go to Drive's bin, where Google normally keeps them for 30 days. To remove Qnote's access and your data:

  1. Stop syncing: press Disconnect in About Qnote on each device. This forgets the sign-in on that device.
  2. Delete from Drive: delete the Qnote folder in your Google Drive, and empty the bin if you want it gone at once.
  3. Revoke access: remove Qnote at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  4. Delete from your device: uninstall Qnote. On Windows, also delete the Qnote folder in your Documents.

There is nothing to delete on the developer's side, because the developer holds none of your data.

Children

Qnote isn't directed at children under 13, and it collects nothing from anyone for its developer.
